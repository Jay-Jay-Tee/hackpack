# Sponsors

**Sponsors** are the companies paying for the event. In return they get to talk at the opening, put their logo on things, meet students, and usually run their own prize. For you, they're a source of prizes, free credits, help and sometimes jobs.

## What sponsors offer

- **Sponsor prizes**, also called sponsor tracks or challenges: "best use of our API", "best project on climate", and so on. Judged by the sponsor's own people, separately from the main prize.
- **Credits and access**: free API credits, cloud credits, hardware to borrow, or early access to a product. See [free tiers](../resources/free-stuff.md).
- **Mentors**: engineers from the company who know their product well and walk round helping.
- **Workshops**: short sessions on using their tools, often in the first few hours.
- **Booths or tables** where you can ask questions, pick up swag and, at bigger events, talk about internships.

## Picking a sponsor prize

Most events let you enter the main prize and one or more sponsor prizes with the same project. Sponsor prizes often get fewer serious entries than the main one, so they can be a better chance of winning something.

Before you choose:

1. **Read the exact requirements** on the event page or the sponsor's slide at the opening. "Use our API" can mean a specific product, plan or feature.
2. **Check what the prize is judged on.** Some sponsors care most about how deeply you used their product; others want a good project that happens to use it.
3. **Pick one or two that fit your idea**, not every track you could technically enter. If their product is only there to qualify, the sponsor's judges will see that straight away.

Decide during [ideas and scoping](../guide/02-ideas-and-scoping.md), not after building. Adding a sponsor's product at the end usually means it isn't part of the main feature, which is what sponsor judges look for.

## Talking to sponsors at the event

Go to the sponsor's table or find their mentors early, ideally on the first day:

- Ask what they'd like to see built with their product, and what a strong entry looks like to them.
- Ask about anything that's confusing in their docs. Helping people use their product is why they're there.
- If you get stuck later, they're often the fastest help available, because they built the thing.

Write down the names of the people you talk to. The same people are often the judges for that prize.

## Showing the sponsor's part

Sponsor judges may see dozens of projects. Make it easy for them to see where their product is.

- **In the pitch**: say the sponsor's product by name, and what it does in your app. "We use Gemini to read the photo and list the ingredients."
- **On the slides**: their logo on the How it works slide, next to what it does. See [slides](slides.md#the-slides-in-order).
- **In the demo**: point to the moment the sponsor's product is doing its work.
- **In the README** and the **submission form**: list every sponsor product you used. Most submission platforms have a field for which prizes you're entering; tick each one.

If the sponsor judges at your table rather than on stage, start the conversation with their part, then show the rest.

## Credits

- **Claim credits early.** They're often given out at the opening or through a form, and some run out.
- **Read what happens when they end.** Some services need a card and start charging after the free credits. Set a spending limit or delete the project after the event.
- **Don't share credit codes or API keys publicly**, including in your repo. See [API keys](../basics/apis.md).

## Recruiting

Sponsors are often there partly to hire. If you're interested:

- Have your GitHub profile and a short CV or LinkedIn page up to date before the event.
- Ask the people at the booth what internships they run and when applications open.
- A working project that used their product is a good thing to mention in an application later.

You don't have to talk to recruiters to enter a sponsor prize, and they won't mind if you're only there for the help.

## Swag

Free t-shirts, stickers and bottles, usually at the sponsor tables. It usually goes quickly, so visit the tables early if you want any.
