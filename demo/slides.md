# Slides

The slides are there to frame the demo, not replace it. Most hackathon pitches need five or six slides, and a judge should be able to read each one in a few seconds.

## Which tool

Use whatever the team can all edit at once. That usually means **Google Slides** or **Canva**, because several people can work on the same deck and nothing needs installing. PowerPoint and Keynote work too, but only one person has the file, so it ends up on their laptop and nobody else can fix a typo.

| Tool | Good for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google Slides | Editing together, free, works anywhere | Needs internet unless you export a copy |
| Canva | Good-looking templates quickly | Templates are busy; delete most of the decoration |
| PowerPoint | Everyone knows it; offline | Fonts can change on another computer |
| Keynote | Mac only, looks clean | Can't be opened on Windows without exporting |

Set the slide size to **16:9**. Almost every projector and screen is that shape now.

## The slides, in order

| # | Slide | What's on it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Title | Project name, a one-line description, team names, track or sponsor prize if any |
| 2 | Problem | Who has the problem and what it costs them. One sentence, maybe a number or a photo |
| 3 | Demo | Just the word "Demo", or a screenshot. You switch to the app here |
| 4 | How it works | The stack, in boxes or a short list, and the one technical part you're proudest of |
| 5 | What's next | Who would use it and the next step |
| 6 | Thanks | Name, live URL and a QR code to it, repo link |

Leave slide 6 up during questions. Judges can scan the QR code and try the app themselves.

If a sponsor prize needs their product, put its logo on the How it works slide and say its name out loud.

## Making them readable

The room is big and the projector is usually worse than your laptop screen. Write for the back row.

- **One idea per slide.** If a slide needs a heading and three paragraphs, it's two slides, or it's something to say rather than show.
- **Big text.** At least 28pt for body text and 40pt for headings. If you need smaller, there's too much on the slide.
- **Few words.** Aim for under 20 words a slide. You say the rest.
- **High contrast.** Dark text on white, or white on near-black. Light grey on white disappears on a projector.
- **Real screenshots** of your app beat stock photos and icons. Crop them to the part that matters.
- **One font**, two at most. Use the slide tool's defaults if nobody on the team cares about design.
- **No animation** apart from simple appearances, if you must. Transitions eat time and sometimes break when exported.

Don't put code on a slide unless it's a few lines and the point of the project. Nobody reads code from across a room.

## Numbers

If you have a real number, use it, something like "A third of students in our survey of 40 skipped lunch to finish labs." If you don't have one, don't make one up. Judges ask where numbers come from.

Say where a statistic comes from in small text on the slide.

## Getting them onto the screen

Find out early how the venue runs presentations. There are usually two setups:

- **You plug in your own laptop.** Bring the adapter for your laptop's port. Most venues have HDMI; newer laptops only have USB-C, so carry a USB-C to HDMI adapter.
- **One shared computer runs every team's slides.** You'll be asked to submit a file or a link beforehand. Send a **PDF**: it looks the same on every computer and can't lose its fonts.

Either way:

1. **Export a PDF** (Google Slides: File → Download → PDF). Keep a copy on your laptop and one on a USB stick or in the team chat.
2. If you share a link instead, set sharing to **anyone with the link can view**, then open it in a private window to check.
3. **Test on the real screen** if you get the chance, especially colours and how small text looks.

If you're presenting from Google Slides with no reliable internet, download it as PowerPoint or PDF and present from the file.

## Speaker notes

Write a few words per slide in the speaker notes, not a script. Reading a script out loud sounds like reading. Presenter view (which shows your notes on your laptop and only the slide on the projector) only works if the screen is set to **extend**, not mirror; see [the live demo](live-demo.md#your-laptop) for that. When switching between slides and a live app, it's often simpler to mirror and skip presenter view.

## Using AI on the slides

AI is useful for cutting text down: paste a slide's text and ask for it in under 15 words. It's bad at deciding what your project's story is, and AI-generated slide images are easy to spot. Keep your own screenshots.
