# The demo video

Many hackathons ask for a short video with the submission, especially online events and ones that use Devpost. Judges often watch these before the live round, or instead of it. Even when it isn't required, record one: it's the backup for your [live demo](live-demo.md).

## Check the rules first

The event page usually says:

- **How long.** Often two or three minutes maximum. Judges stop watching at the limit.
- **Where to upload.** Usually a YouTube or Vimeo link. Some accept Google Drive or a file upload.
- **What it must show.** Some want the team on camera, or a pitch as well as the demo.

## Plan it

One to two minutes is enough. A simple structure:

1. **The problem**, in one or two sentences (10–15 seconds).
2. **The demo path**, the same one as the [live demo](live-demo.md#pick-one-path) (60–90 seconds).
3. **How it's built**, in a sentence, and the live URL (10–15 seconds).

Write the voice-over as a short script and read it a couple of times before recording. A script in your own words sounds better than improvising, and much better than one written by an AI.

## Recording your screen

Record at the size the judges will watch it: make the browser window large, zoom in to 125% or so, and close anything personal.

| System | Built-in | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Windows 11 | Snipping Tool | `Win+Shift+S`, switch to the video camera icon, drag over the area, press Start |
| Windows | Xbox Game Bar | `Win+Alt+R` starts and stops recording the current app |
| macOS | Screenshot toolbar | `Cmd+Shift+5`, choose "Record Entire Screen" or "Record Selected Portion"; Options → pick your microphone |
| Linux (GNOME) | Screenshot tool | `Print Screen`, switch to the video camera icon; or `Ctrl+Alt+Shift+R` on older versions |

The macOS recorder doesn't record sound unless you pick a microphone in **Options**.

**[OBS Studio](https://obsproject.com)** works on all three, is free, and can put your webcam in a corner of the screen recording. It takes ten minutes to set up, so do that before the last day.

## Sound

Bad sound is the most common problem with hackathon videos. Judges forgive a plain video but stop watching one they can't hear.

- Record in the quietest place you can find. Hackathon halls are loud; a stairwell or an empty room is better.
- A phone headset or earphones with a mic are usually clearer than the laptop's microphone.
- Speak a little slower than normal.
- **Listen back to the first ten seconds** before recording the rest.

An easy alternative: record the screen with no sound, then record the voice-over separately while watching it, and combine the two in an editor.

## Editing

Keep it to cutting the start and end, and removing long waits.

- **Windows**: Clipchamp (built in on Windows 11).
- **macOS**: iMovie, or QuickTime (Edit → Trim) for simple cuts.
- **Any system**: [DaVinci Resolve](https://www.blackmagicdesign.com/products/davinciresolve) or [Shotcut](https://shotcut.org), both free.

If something takes 10 seconds to load, cut the wait. Add captions if you have time; some judges watch with the sound off.

## Uploading

1. Export as **MP4, 1080p**.
2. Upload to YouTube as **Unlisted** (anyone with the link can watch, but it won't appear in search). Not Private: judges won't be able to open it.
3. Open the link in a private window to check it plays.
4. Paste it into the submission.

Upload at least an hour before the deadline. Big uploads on busy venue Wi-Fi can take a long time, and YouTube needs time to process the video after uploading. If the Wi-Fi is slow, use a phone hotspot.

Keep the MP4 on your laptop as well, for the live demo backup.
